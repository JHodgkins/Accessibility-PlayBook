# Roles and responsibilities

**Audience:** Sponsors, Product, Delivery, Design, Engineering, QA, Accessibility  
**Purpose:** Make ownership explicit so accessibility does not become a QA-only responsibility.  
**When to use:** Planning work, resolving ownership gaps and making release decisions.  
**Owner:** Product / Accessibility

| Role | Core responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Executive sponsor | Provides authority, funding and escalation support |
| Product owner | Ensures accessibility is represented in scope, priority and acceptance |
| Business analyst | Converts user and accessibility needs into requirements |
| Designer | Prevents barriers through accessible interaction and visual design |
| Developer | Implements accessible behaviour and correct semantics |
| QA | Verifies acceptance criteria and reports user-impacting failures |
| Accessibility specialist | Provides expert guidance, complex evaluation and scalable enablement |
| Design system | Builds accessibility into reusable components and patterns |
| Delivery manager | Ensures checkpoints and evidence are completed |
| UAT | Validates key journeys from a user-facing perspective |
| Risk / compliance | Supports governance and exception handling |

## Principle: specialists enable; teams own

The accessibility specialist should not become the only person capable of finding or
fixing accessibility problems. The operating model deliberately moves routine
accessibility responsibility into ordinary product roles while retaining specialist
support for complex decisions.

## Decision ownership

Use one accountable role for each decision. Responsible roles do the work; consulted
roles contribute expertise; informed roles receive the decision and evidence. Names
must be recorded on the work item; a role label alone cannot accept an exception.

| Decision or deliverable | Accountable | Responsible | Consulted | Informed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product accessibility scope | Product owner | Business analyst | Accessibility, procurement, legal | Delivery team |
| Design and content handoff | Design lead | Designers, content designers | Design system, accessibility | Engineering, QA |
| Accessible implementation | Technical lead | Developers | Design system, accessibility | Product, QA |
| Evaluation evidence | QA lead | QA and accessibility test engineers | Developers, UAT | Product, delivery |
| Release with an exception | Authorised risk owner | Product owner | Accessibility, legal, QA | Sponsor, support |
| Training and pattern improvements | Engineering or design lead | Learning team, design system | Accessibility, content design | Delivery teams |

Architects must review platform constraints before procurement or implementation.
Procurement must request supplier evidence and contractual remediation routes.
Content designers own understandable labels, instructions and recovery messages.
Learning teams should turn recurring failures into role-specific exercises, with
practice and feedback rather than completion counts alone.

### Example handoff

For a registration form, the analyst records error-recovery criteria; the designer
annotates the error summary; the developer links errors to fields; QA attaches a
keyboard and screen-reader record. Product accepts only when the evidence matches
the criteria, or routes an unresolved barrier through the exception process.
