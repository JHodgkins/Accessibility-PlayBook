# Escalation and exceptions

An exception is not a way to make an accessibility defect disappear.

A useful exception record identifies:

- the barrier;
- the affected users and journeys;
- severity and business impact;
- available workaround, if any;
- why remediation cannot occur before release;
- remediation owner and target date;
- authorised approver;
- review or expiry date.

Exceptions should be visible, time-bound and reviewed. Repeated exceptions for the
same pattern are a signal that a systemic fix is needed.

## Example review and expiry

**Audience:** Product owners, delivery managers and authorised risk owners  
**Purpose:** Keep temporary release decisions accountable.

Illustrative record EX-04 concerns a secondary export whose column headings are
missing. Product proposes an accessible HTML view as a temporary alternative. QA
must verify that it supplies the same information without extra cost or permissions.
The risk owner may approve only within the organisation's delegated authority.

- Decision: permit one release on 5 October 2026, with the defect still open.
- Remediation owner: reporting team lead; fix due 12 October.
- Review: 9 October, including use of the alternative and any new user impact.
- Expiry: 16 October; no automatic extension.
- Closure: QA retests the export and records evidence; the approver closes the exception.

If the workaround fails or the impact changes, escalate immediately. If the fix misses
expiry, block subsequent affected releases pending a fresh authorised decision.
An accepted risk does not establish compliance. See [risk acceptance](/delivery/risk-acceptance).
