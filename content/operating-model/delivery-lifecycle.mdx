# Accessibility delivery lifecycle

**Audience:** Product owners, delivery managers, designers, developers, QA  
**Purpose:** Define when accessibility work happens and prevent end-of-cycle surprises.  
**When to use:** Planning checkpoints and reviewing readiness for the next stage.  
**Owner:** Delivery / Product

## 1. Discovery

Identify disabled-user needs, applicable standards, high-risk journeys and likely
assistive-technology interactions.

**Evidence:** user needs, accessibility risks, research questions.

## 2. Requirements and refinement

Create testable accessibility acceptance criteria and identify dependencies on
design-system components or specialist review.

**Evidence:** backlog criteria, Definition of Done, identified risks.

## 3. Design

Review interaction states, focus behaviour, error handling, responsive behaviour,
content structure and reusable patterns.

**Evidence:** annotated designs or approved component pattern.

## 4. Development

Use native semantics where possible, implement keyboard support, accessible names,
states and focus management, then perform developer checks.

**Evidence:** code review, automated scan where appropriate, developer test notes.

## 5. QA and accessibility evaluation

Combine automated checks with manual keyboard, screen-reader, zoom/magnification and
other relevant testing.

**Evidence:** test results, defects, traceability to acceptance criteria.

## 6. UAT

Exercise realistic end-to-end journeys. UAT does not replace specialist testing; it
adds confidence that important workflows remain understandable and operable.

## 7. Release decision

Review unresolved accessibility risk alongside other product risks.

Critical barriers to core journeys should normally block release unless the
organisation's authorised exception process explicitly decides otherwise.

## 8. Post-release

Monitor customer feedback, regressions and recurring defect patterns. Feed lessons
back into patterns, training and automated controls.

## The feedback loop

A repeated defect should not only be fixed in one product. Ask whether the
**component, pattern, developer guidance, test guidance or training** also needs to
change.

## Lifecycle at a glance

<ol className="lifecycle">
  <li><strong>Discover:</strong> understand users and barriers.</li>
  <li><strong>Define:</strong> agree outcomes and acceptance criteria.</li>
  <li><strong>Design:</strong> describe every interaction state.</li>
  <li><strong>Build:</strong> implement and self-check.</li>
  <li><strong>Evaluate:</strong> test and remediate.</li>
  <li><strong>Validate:</strong> exercise realistic user journeys.</li>
  <li><strong>Release:</strong> record the evidence and decision.</li>
  <li><strong>Learn:</strong> feed findings into the next discovery cycle.</li>
</ol>

The ordered list is the diagram's text equivalent: progress follows the list, and
post-release learning returns to discovery. Teams may iterate within any stage.

## Worked evidence trail: account registration

| Stage | Example artefact | Review before proceeding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery | Research note: users magnify fields and lose error context | Product identifies recovery as a high-risk task |
| Requirements | Story REG-12 with field naming and error recovery criteria | QA confirms each outcome can be tested |
| Design | Annotated normal, invalid and success states | Design and engineering agree focus destinations |
| Development | Review notes and component checks | Technical lead verifies native controls and relationships |
| QA | Environment log, keyboard record and defect A11Y-017 | QA links failures to the story criteria |
| UAT | Participant task outcomes and consented observations | Product reviews unresolved journey barriers |
| Release | Report with fixed defects, gaps and signed decisions | Named approver records release or hold |
| Post-release | Support trend: repeated address errors | Product funds a component improvement and training |

Store durable links, dates, build identifiers and reviewer names. Do not attach
personal participant data or credentials to broadly accessible tickets.
