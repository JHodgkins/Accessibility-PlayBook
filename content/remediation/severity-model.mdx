# Accessibility severity model

**Audience:** QA, Product, Accessibility, Engineering, Delivery  
**Purpose:** Create consistent prioritisation without equating WCAG level with business severity.  
**When to use:** Defect triage, prioritisation and release review.  
**Owner:** Product / QA, with Accessibility consulted

## Critical

Prevents a disabled user completing a core or safety/business-critical journey and no
reasonable workaround exists.

**Example:** A keyboard user cannot activate the only control that submits a payment.

## High

Causes a major loss of functionality or independence. A workaround may exist but is
difficult, unreliable or requires substantial additional effort.

**Example:** A modal traps screen-reader focus away from the controls needed to continue.

## Medium

Creates a meaningful accessibility barrier but the task remains achievable with
moderate additional effort or an effective workaround.

**Example:** Error text is not programmatically associated with a field but an error
summary clearly links back to the field.

## Low

Creates limited friction, inconsistency or a lower-impact issue that does not
materially block the task.

## Severity decision prompts

Ask:

- Which users are affected?
- Is a core journey blocked?
- Is functionality completely unavailable or only harder?
- Is there a reliable workaround?
- How frequently will users encounter the barrier?
- Does it create safety, financial, legal or privacy consequences?
- Is the problem systemic across many components or pages?

WCAG conformance level is relevant context, but it should not be the sole severity
algorithm.

## Triage and review

QA proposes severity with a reproducible user-impact statement. Product confirms
journey criticality and the accessibility specialist reviews disputed or unfamiliar
barriers. Record the rationale and any disagreement; escalate rather than silently
lowering severity to meet a release date.

Severity describes impact. Priority adds scheduling, reach and dependencies. A Low
severity defect can still fail an applicable requirement and must have an owner.
Validate workarounds with the relevant access method; “ask someone else” does not
preserve independent access. Reassess when new evidence changes the impact.
